Introduction to Safe Practices

Now that you understand how attackers use social engineering tactics, it's time to learn practical actions you can take to protect yourself. This module focuses on four core defensive practices that remote workers can apply immediately to reduce their risk.

You'll learn how to:

  • Enable secure multi-factor authentication (MFA) on your accounts.
  • Create and manage strong passwords.
  • Verify suspicious requests before responding.
  • Report potential social engineering attempts.

Topic 1: Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

What is Multi-Factor Authentication?

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is a security method that requires you to provide two or more pieces of evidence to prove you are who you claim to be. Instead of just entering a password, MFA adds an extra layer of protection by requiring something you know (password) plus something you have (phone, authenticator app) or something you are (fingerprint).

Even if an attacker steals your password through phishing or other social engineering tactics, they cannot access your account without the second factor. This dramatically reduces the risk of unauthorized access.

Why MFA Matters for Remote Workers?

Remote workers often access sensitive company data from various locations and devices. This increased exposure makes your accounts an attractive target for attackers. MFA is one of the most effective defenses because it requires attackers to have physical access to your second factor, like your phone, in addition to knowing your password.

Many remote work platforms now require or strongly recommend MFA. By enabling it on all your accounts, you protect not just yourself but also the organizations you work with.

Common MFA Methods

SMS Text Messages

A code is sent to your phone via text. This method is widely available but can be vulnerable to SIM swapping attacks where attackers trick your phone company into transferring your number to a new device.

Authenticator Apps

Apps like Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator generate time-based codes on your phone. These are more secure than SMS because the codes are generated locally on your device and cannot be intercepted like text messages.

Email Verification

A code is sent to your email address. This is convenient but only as secure as your email account, which could be compromised if attackers gain access to it.

Biometric Authentication

Fingerprint or facial recognition. This is highly secure because it's unique to you and cannot be stolen or guessed.

Topic 2: Password Security

What Makes a Strong Password?

A strong password is one that is difficult for attackers to guess or crack using automated tools. Strong passwords typically have at least 12 characters and include a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters such as @, #, $, and %.

Weak passwords like "password123" or "123456" can be cracked in seconds. Strong passwords like "Tr0pic@lSunset#2024" would take years for attackers to crack through brute force attacks.

Why Password Security Matters for Remote Workers?

Remote workers use passwords to access email, cloud storage, project management tools, banking systems, and more. If attackers crack one password, they may try the same password on your other accounts.

Strong, unique passwords for each account ensure that if one account is compromised, your other accounts remain safe.

Password Security: The Best Practices

Use a Unique Password for Each Account

Never reuse the same password across multiple accounts. If attackers get your password from one service, they cannot access your other accounts.

Avoid Personal Information

Don't use names, birthdays, or other personal information that can be found on social media or through a quick search.

Avoid Dictionary Words

Don't use common words or predictable patterns. Attackers use dictionary attacks that try common words first.

Use a Password Manager

Password managers like Bitwarden, 1Password, or LastPass can securely store and generate strong passwords for you, so you don't have to remember them all.

Change Passwords Regularly

Update important passwords every 90 days, especially if you suspect compromise.

Topic 3: Verifying Suspicious Requests

What Does It Mean to Verify a Request?

Verification means taking steps to confirm that a request such as an email, phone call, or message is actually coming from who it claims to be from.

Before you respond to any request for sensitive information or action, you should verify the sender's identity through an independent method.

Why Verification Matters for Remote Workers?

Remote workers communicate primarily through digital channels like email, chat, and video calls. This makes it easier for attackers to impersonate colleagues, managers, or vendors without face-to-face contact to confirm identity.

A single verification step can prevent credential theft, financial fraud, or data breaches.

How to Verify Suspicious Requests

For Email Requests

If an email asks you to click a link or provide information, don't use the link in the email. Instead, go directly to the official website by typing the URL yourself or use a bookmark.

For Phone Calls

If someone calls claiming to be from your bank or company, hang up and call the official phone number. Never use a phone number provided by the caller.

For Messages or Chat

If a colleague asks for sensitive information through chat, verify by asking them in person or through a different communication channel.

Check the Sender's Email Address

Look carefully at the full email address, not just the display name. Attackers often use addresses that look similar to legitimate ones.

Look for Urgency and Pressure

Legitimate organizations rarely pressure you to act immediately without verification. Pressure to act fast can be a warning sign.

Topic 4: Reporting Social Engineering Attempts

Why Should You Report Social Engineering Attempts?

Reporting suspicious emails, calls, or messages helps protect not just yourself, but also your organization and colleagues.

When you report an attack, security teams can investigate the threat, block the attacker, and alert other potential victims.

Why Reporting Matters for Remote Workers?

Remote workers are often the first line of defense against cyberattacks. Because you work from personal devices and networks, you may encounter threats that office-based security systems never see.

Your reports of suspicious activity provide critical information to your organization's security team.

How to Report Social Engineering Attempts

Report Phishing Emails

Most email platforms have a "Report Phishing" or "Report Spam" button. Use it instead of simply deleting the email.

Report to Your Organization

If you work for a company, report suspicious activity through its security team or official help desk process.

Report Suspicious Calls

If you receive a vishing call, note the phone number, what was said, and when the call occurred, then report it through your organization's official security process.

Report to Authorities

If you believe you've been a victim of fraud or identity theft, report it to the appropriate local authorities.

Don't Engage Further

Once you suspect an attack, stop engaging with the attacker. Don't respond, call back, or provide any additional information.

Real-World Application Checklist

Now that you've learned about Safe Practices, here are concrete actions you can take immediately to protect yourself.

Do This Today

  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) on at least one important account.
  • Review one of your current passwords and change it if it doesn't meet the recommended requirements.
  • Test your verification skills by carefully checking the sender address of a recent email.

Do This Week

  • Enable MFA on all your important accounts.
  • Update passwords for your most important accounts to strong, unique passwords.
  • Set up a password manager to securely store and generate passwords.
  • Locate your organization's security contact information and save it for future reference.

Moving Forward

  • Pause before clicking links or opening attachments from unknown senders.
  • Verify suspicious requests through another communication channel.
  • Report suspicious emails, calls, or messages.
  • Update important passwords when compromise is suspected.
  • Stay informed about new social engineering tactics.
