Introduction to Phishing

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where cybercriminals impersonate legitimate organizations to trick people into revealing sensitive information.

Key Concepts to Know

Understanding these core concepts will help you identify phishing attempts:

Email Spoofing

Forging the sender's email address to make a malicious email appear to come from a trusted source (e.g., paypa1.com instead of paypal.com).

Credential Harvesting

Tricking users into entering their username, password, or other sensitive information on fake login pages that look legitimate.

Malware Distribution

Sending attachments or links that, when clicked, install malicious software on your device such as viruses, spyware, or ransomware.

Social Engineering

Manipulating people into divulging confidential information by exploiting trust, urgency, fear, or authority.

Vishing and Smishing

Voice phishing through calls and SMS phishing through text messages extend phishing attacks beyond email to phones and messaging platforms.

Real-World Examples

Learn from actual examples of phishing attacks and how to spot them.

Example 1: The Bank Account Verification

What happened: Employees received emails claiming their bank account needed immediate verification due to "suspicious activity."

Red flags: Generic greeting, urgent language, link to external website, request for sensitive information.

Why it worked: Banks do send security alerts, so this seemed legitimate at first glance.

Example 2: The Fake IT Support Request

What happened: Employees received emails from "IT Support" asking them to update their password due to a system upgrade.

Red flags: Legitimate IT would never ask for passwords via email, generic subject line, urgency tactic.

Why it worked: Most employees are used to IT requests and didn't question it.

Example 3: The Compromised Colleague

What happened: An email appeared to come from a colleague asking to urgently transfer funds to a new vendor account.

Red flags: Unusual request from colleague, request for secrecy, unusual urgency, new vendor account.

Why it worked: The attacker compromised the colleague's email account first.

Example 4: The Prize Notification

What happened: Employees received text messages saying they won a free gift card and needed to "claim prize now" through a link.

Red flags: Unsolicited prize, shortened URL, urgency tactic, and no expectation of winning.

Why it worked: People got excited about a "free" item without thinking.
