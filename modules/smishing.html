Introduction to Smishing

Smishing is phishing conducted through SMS (Short Message Service) text messages. Because text messages feel more personal and trustworthy than emails, smishing attacks often have higher success rates than traditional email phishing.

Key Concepts to Know

Understanding these core concepts will help you identify smishing attempts:

Delivery and Logistics Scams

Fake text messages claiming packages cannot be delivered and asking users to click a link to reschedule delivery or pay fees. These mimic legitimate shipping notifications.

Banking and Financial Alerts

Fraudulent SMS messages pretending to be from banks warning of suspicious account activity and requesting verification of personal or financial information through a malicious link.

Prize and Raffle Notifications

Scam text messages claiming the recipient has won a prize, gift card, or raffle and must click a link to claim it within a limited time, creating false urgency.

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Bypasses

Attacker-generated SMS messages mimicking legitimate 2FA codes or asking users to verify their identity, tricking them into revealing actual authentication codes sent by legitimate services.

Shortened URLs and Link Obfuscation

Using shortened URL services (bit.ly, tinyurl) to hide the true destination of malicious links, making it impossible to see the actual website being accessed until after clicking.

Real-World Examples

Learn from actual examples of smishing attacks and how to spot them.

Example 1: The Fake Delivery Notification

What happened: A remote worker received a text saying "Your package could not be delivered. Click here to reschedule: bit.ly/delivery123" from what appeared to be a delivery company.

Red flags: Shortened URL hiding real destination, urgency to act, request to click unfamiliar link, message arriving unexpectedly without prior notification.

Why it worked: The victim was expecting a delivery and the message felt legitimate because package delivery notifications are common.

Example 2: The Bank Alert Scam

What happened: An employee received a text: "Your bank account has unusual activity. Verify your identity here: [link]" with the bank's logo in the message header.

Red flags: Banks don't ask for verification via text, suspicious link in message, generic greeting, pressure to act quickly.

Why it worked: People trust text messages from what appears to be their bank more than email, and the message created urgency.

Example 3: The Prize Notification

What happened: Multiple people received identical texts: "Congratulations! You won a $500 Amazon gift card! Claim now: tinyurl.com/prize" with 24-hour expiration.

Red flags: Unsolicited prize claim, limited-time offer creating urgency, shortened URL, identical message to many recipients, no prior contest entry.

Why it worked: The promise of free money and fake time pressure made people act without thinking critically.

Example 4: The 2FA Code Trick

What happened: A freelancer received a text: "Your 2FA code is 246891. If you didn't request this, click here to deny" from what looked like their email service.

Red flags: Legitimate services never ask to "deny" verification via link, suspicious request to click link related to security codes.

Why it worked: The attacker had compromised the user's email password and was requesting 2FA codes while simultaneously sending a fake denial link to intercept the legitimate code.
