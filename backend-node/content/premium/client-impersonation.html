What to know

Why freelancers get targeted

A freelancer has no , no in-house legal team and nobody doing security for them. Income depends on landing the next project, so the fear of losing a good one is always there. Attackers build a convincing “high-budget client” persona for exactly that reason: to make you drop your usual checks (Ticona, 2022).

Key takeaway

A promising project can make an unusual request feel reasonable. Keep the same verification routine even when the offer is attractive.

What to know

Portfolio credential harvesting

Not mass phishing. The pretext is a commercial brief written for you.

  • The review portal. A client interested in a high-value contract sends a link to a “prototype review portal” or “shared client workspace” — figma-client-verify.com, adobe-portfolio-review.site. Sign in with your professional account to see the brief, they say.
  • The client’s own system. You are asked to log into their , or cloud repository using they supply. The login page is a harvester, built to capture your main password and your .

Why a familiar logo is not enough

A fake page can copy a logo, colors and layout. Open the service through your own bookmark or type its known address yourself. Treat the example addresses in this lesson as fictional simulations; do not visit them.

Key takeaway

A realistic brief can be bait. Verify an unfamiliar portal independently before entering your password or code.

What to know

Getting you off the platform

Upwork, Fiverr and Toptal log the contract, verify identities and hold payment in . So the first move is to get you talking somewhere else.

Understand the protection your arrangement provides

Payment and dispute protections vary by platform and contract. Check your actual agreement. Moving to a different channel does not verify the client or establish that a payment is genuine.

Within the agreed platform process

  • Documented contract and messages
  • Available identity checks
  • Payment protections under the platform’s terms

Pressure to bypass that process

  • Unverified promises
  • Reduced platform protection
  • Requests for irreversible payments
  • The convenience story. Their accounting department cannot pay through the platform, and there is a 30% bonus if you accept a direct wire, PayPal Friends & Family, or cryptocurrency.
  • The overpayment. An advance check or wire arrives for more than the agreed fee. An accounting error, they say — please return the difference now, by a method that cannot be reversed. The original payment is later flagged as fraudulent, leaving you owing what you refunded.

Key takeaway

Do not let a bonus or urgent start replace verification. If an unexpected overpayment arrives, contact your payment provider through its official channel before moving money.

You are a freelance web developer. “Marcus Vance” emails you, says he is Marketing Director at an international agency, and offers a $12,000 retainer to redesign their core web platform.

Red flags & checks

Red flags

  • A client who needs you to log into an unfamiliar third-party portal to read the spec or submit work.
  • An early push to move the conversation, or the payment, off an -protected platform.
  • An advance for more than the agreed amount, with a request to refund the difference.
  • A major agency writing from a lookalike domain or a free webmail account.

Verify a client out of band

  1. Find the company’s official website yourself rather than following a link in the message.
  2. Contact the company through independently found details to confirm the person and project.
  3. Compare the sender’s full domain with the official company domain.
  4. Confirm the scope, contract and payment arrangement through the platform’s own messages and records.

Replying “Are you real?” to the same email or calling a number supplied in it does not provide independent verification.

Do’s and don’ts

Do

  • Verify through a separate trusted channel.
  • Keep a record of agreed scope and payment.
  • Grant only necessary, authorized access.

Don’t

  • Share your password or code.
  • Let urgency replace checks.
  • Refund an unexpected excess payment under pressure.

Module key takeaways

  • A high-value offer does not establish identity.
  • Read the full domain before signing in.
  • Verify independently before granting access or delivering work.
  • Keep agreed contract and payment protections in place.
  • Ask your payment provider about unexpected overpayments before acting.
